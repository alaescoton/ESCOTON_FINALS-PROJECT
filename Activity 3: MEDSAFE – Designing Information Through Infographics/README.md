# ACTIVITY DESCRIPTION °❀.ೃ࿔ 
This activity allowed me to apply visual communication and information design by creating an infographic introducing MEDSAFE: A Medication Safety and Verification System. The infographic focused on concerns surrounding misleading medication claims, unverified products, and self-medication, particularly involving Ozempic and tirzepatide. It presented MEDSAFE as a proposed technology-enabled system that helps users learn about medications, check claims encountered online, verify product and source information, and seek appropriate healthcare guidance. My goal was to transform a complex healthcare-related concept into a concise, organized, and visually understandable material that could educate people about the importance of medication safety.

# WHAT I HAVE LEARNED °❀.ೃ࿔
Through this activity, I learned that graphic design can serve a meaningful purpose beyond aesthetics. An infographic can simplify complicated information, highlight important concerns, and help readers understand a topic more quickly. I also developed skills in information organization, visual storytelling, content prioritization, and translating technical healthcare concepts into language that is easier for the general public to understand. Most importantly, I realized that the accuracy of the information is just as important as the appearance of the design, especially when communicating health-related topics.

One challenge I encountered was deciding which information should be included because medication safety involves several important considerations. Including too much information could make the infographic difficult to read, while removing too much could affect the completeness of the message. I addressed this by identifying the most essential points and organizing them into four main functions: Learn, Check, Verify, and Consult. I also made sure to communicate that MEDSAFE is an educational and information-support concept, not a system that diagnoses conditions or prescribes medication.

This activity is particularly relevant to my future nursing profession because patient education and health promotion require the ability to communicate reliable information effectively. I can apply these skills when creating medication guides, patient education materials, health awareness campaigns, and community health presentations. Moving forward, I want to improve my ability to combine evidence-based information with accessible visual communication so that the materials I create can contribute to informed and safer healthcare decisions.
